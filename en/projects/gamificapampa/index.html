The idea

GamificaPampa brings together outreach projects from UNIPAMPA's Alegrete campus that take gamification and artificial intelligence to K-12 teachers, in partnership with public schools in Alegrete, Brazil, through two projects: Educador 5.0, on AI, and GamificaEdu, on gamification. The site treats teacher training as a game: teachers go through three levels, Discover, Create and Apply, until they have a gamified lesson ready for their class.

My part is the teaching resources, the things that end up in the hands of teachers and kids. In the games, kids build command sequences, hunt bugs, pair program and find out how an app talks to a server and a database. For teachers, the Prompt Workshop shows how to use Gemini to create activities, stories and even games for their own classes, with no programming required.

Design decisions

  1. QR codes for the class

    The class menu has a short address to write on the board, gamificapampa.github.io/turma, and a big QR code to project. Resources have a Share button that sends the link through WhatsApp or Google Classroom.

  2. Ready to print

    The lesson plan, activity sheets, level maps and certificates come out as A4 PDFs, generated from the pages themselves.

  3. No sign-up

    No game asks for a login, and the site collects no data from the kids. Just open and play, on a phone, a tablet or the projector.

  4. Accessible

    Checked color contrast, zoom allowed and larger touch targets in the faster games.

From a personal project to the organization

The games and the Workshop started in a repository of mine, on my own GitHub. When they became part of GamificaPampa, they moved to the GamificaPampa organization on GitHub, each piece in the right repository: what teachers and kids use stayed public in recursos-pedagogicos, and the kit for whoever runs the training went to a private team repository.

The most important rule of the move was not to break a single address. There were already QR codes printed on booths, slides, posters and invitations, and links sent over WhatsApp and Classroom. So every old address got a new destination in a table, a script checks all of them, and the old site becomes a bridge that sends each link to the right place. The move happens through pull requests, one phase at a time, and each phase only starts once the previous one is live.
